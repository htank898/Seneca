How the numbers are made

  1. Start with today’s base. 18,600 Pro seats on Jan 1, 2027 in both paths. Seats leave at the monthly churn rate: seats next month = seats × (1 − churn). Churn is logo churn; at an average of 15 seats per account, seats leave in proportion.
  2. Hold path. Churn stays at 1.6% every month. MRR = seats × $49.
  3. Scenario path. Churn is 1.6% plus your extra churn in months 1–6, then 1.6% for months 7–12. Everyone pays the discounted price all year: MRR = seats × new price × (1 − discount).
  4. Year-1 ARR change is the 12 monthly differences added up: Σ (scenario MRR − hold MRR), Jan through Dec 2027. That is the extra recurring revenue the change brings in during its first year.
  5. Break-even extra churn is the first-6-months extra churn at which that sum falls to $0, found by searching between 0 and 50 points. Below it, the increase pays off in year 1.

Why everyone reprices on Jan 1

  • In reality, existing customers move at their next renewal after Jan 1, so the real increase phases in through 2027.
  • Repricing all seats at once keeps the arithmetic short enough to check by hand, and it front-loads the churn risk — a deliberately cautious read of the downside.
  • Because each customer’s first year at the new price is discounted, all of 2027 is billed at the discounted rate here. The full list price arrives in 2028.

Not in this model

  • New customers, who would pay full list price from day one.
  • Seat expansion inside existing accounts, and plan up- or downgrades.
  • Margin: at a higher price, each seat also carries more room for AI triage costs.
