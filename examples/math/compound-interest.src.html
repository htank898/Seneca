Money math, one page

Compound interest

Interest that earns interest. One formula covers savings accounts, loans and index funds. Everything else on this page is a rearrangement of it.

$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$$
$A$
what you end up with
$P$
what you put in
$r$
annual rate, as a decimal
$n$
compounding periods per year
$t$
years

Compounding all the time

As $n \to \infty$, the bracket converges to $e^{r}$ for each year:

\[ A = P e^{rt} \]

The rule of 72

Doubling time, solved from $2P = P(1+r)^t$:

$$t_{2\times} = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1+r)} \approx \frac{72}{100\,r}$$

Monthly contributions

Add $c$ every month at monthly rate $i = r/12$, for $m$ months:

$$FV = c \cdot \frac{(1+i)^{m} - 1}{i}$$

The rate you actually get

The annual percentage yield folds compounding into one number:

\text{APY} = \left(1 + \tfrac{r}{n}\right)^{n} - 1

Worked example

Put \$10,000 in at 5% compounded monthly for 30 years:

$$ A = 10{,}000\left(1 + \frac{0.05}{12}\right)^{12 \cdot 30} \approx \$44{,}677 $$

The same money at simple interest grows to only $10{,}000\,(1 + 0.05 \cdot 30) = \$25{,}000$. The gap of about \$19,677 is interest on interest.
